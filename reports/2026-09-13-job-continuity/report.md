# Detailed analytical report

As of September 13, 2026. Read the [audit and definitions](evidence.md) before interpreting numbers. Recommendations below are **proposals** unless explicitly identified as findings. Sources outside the database are current official research, not ledger-backed observations.

## 1. Demand, employment and early risks

**Finding:** Michigan's July 2026 seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was 4.9%. Employment fell 23,000 and the labor force fell 29,000 over the month. A declining unemployment rate therefore does not by itself establish improving employment opportunity. Detroit metropolitan unemployment was 5.3%. These are household-survey measures, distinct from establishment payroll jobs. [Michigan July release](https://www.michigan.gov/mcda/labor-market-information/employment-situation-press-releases/2026/08/20/michigan-jobless-rate-decreases-during-july).

**Finding:** the following statewide screening benchmarks come from the 2026 Career Outlook, with projections for 2024–2034. Hourly ranges are published 25th–75th percentiles, not entry offers. Annual openings include replacement demand and are not active vacancies. The publication warns that favorable outlook does not necessarily mean current hiring demand. [Official Career Outlook](https://www.michigan.gov/mcda/-/media/Project/Websites/mcda/reports/2026/Michigan-Career-Outlook-through-2034.pdf).

| Occupation | Annual openings | Growth | Hourly range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Industrial engineers | 2,035 | 5.7% | $40–61 |
| Mechanical engineers | 1,845 | 7.0% | $42–64 |
| Electrical engineers | 605 | 3.5% | $42–64 |
| CNC tool programmers | 250 | 7.9% | $28–37 |
| Industrial machinery mechanics | 1,900 | 13.0% | $28–37 |
| Software developers | 2,445 | 7.7% | $47–67 |
| Registered nurses | 6,505 | 8.3% | $39–50 |
| Electricians | 2,450 | 5.6% | $24–44 |
| Heavy truck drivers | 6,650 | 2.0% | $23–30 |
| Home health/personal care aides | 15,635 | 15.8% | $15–18 |
| Logisticians | 1,120 | 12.8% | $31–51 |
| Service sales representatives | 2,740 | −0.7% | $24–50 |

**Inference:** maintenance offers a useful adjacent-skills hypothesis; engineering provides higher-wage pathways for appropriately qualified workers; care and transportation warrant inclusion because an industrial-only program would miss substantial potential opportunity. Service sales illustrates why openings can remain positive despite projected net decline. A large number of openings in a low-paying occupation is not evidence that it advances the wage-growth mission. Screen total earnings, stable hours, benefits and travel costs before recommending it.

**Missing:** a complete growth-versus-replacement decomposition, current occupation-level vacancies, employer-validated shortages, and regional demand counts. The state projection page offers statewide 2024–2034 files but regional links with 2022–2032 vintages. Workbook downloads returned access errors during this review. Do not combine those vintages into a single ranking or assign a regional shortage total. [Official projection inventory](https://www.michigan.gov/mcda/labor-market-information/employment-projections).

**Proposal:** operate a weekly early-warning review with voluntary employers. Record order-book changes, reduced shifts/overtime, contract end dates, planned automation, supplier distress, hiring freezes and internal openings. These are potential signals, not proof of layoffs. Require a human review and employer confirmation before worker-level outreach; protect confidential commercial information.

| Signal | Proposed action | Evidence required to escalate |
|---|---|---|
| Employer expects surplus labor in 8–12 weeks | Confidential retention assessment within five business days | Employer forecast, affected job families and feasible work alternatives |
| Credible reduction within 30–60 days | Open consented cases; reserve receiving interviews | Dated management confirmation; union consultation where applicable |
| Notice or imminent separation | Rapid Response and benefits navigation immediately | Worker/employer notice; independently verify job offers |
| Receiving employer cannot fill a role | Diagnose pay, shift and skill barriers before training | Funded requisition, recruitment history, actual rejection reasons |

WARN notices provide a public signal for covered events, but are neither a complete layoff census nor an early guarantee: coverage and exceptions matter. The program should collect voluntary signals before a statutory notice becomes necessary. Use the official notice list with a filing-date cutoff; a future separation date is different from a newly filed notice. No comprehensive as-of-date WARN count was established here. [Michigan WARN](https://www.michigan.gov/leo/bureaus-agencies/wd/programs-services/worker-adjustment-and-retraining-notification-act-warn), [notice inventory](https://www.michigan.gov/leo/bureaus-agencies/wd/data-public-notices/warn-notices).

## 2. Skills and practical transitions

The [occupation matrix](matrices.md) separates transferable-skill hypotheses, remaining requirements, provisional training envelopes and wage benchmarks. Employer interviews and work samples must convert each hypothesis into a worker-specific plan. Occupational similarity and CIP–SOC links are discovery aids, not proof of readiness.

**Proposal:** a case assessment records tasks the worker can demonstrate; equipment/software used; credentials and expiration; work history; literacy and numeracy support; accessible training needs; desired pay/hours; benefits; commute; and voluntary mobility preferences. Compare those with a receiving employer's essential tasks. Remove preferences that are not genuinely essential instead of training workers to satisfy unnecessary screening requirements. Do not infer an individual's capability from their institution, age or neighborhood.

For every placement pathway, obtain a written training quote covering tuition, exam fees, equipment, schedule, prerequisites and next available seat. Compare the worker's present regular and overtime earnings with the actual proposed offer, including hours, premiums, insurance waiting periods and travel expense. If the offer lowers earnings, disclose that explicitly and keep searching or use a lawful, funded bridge; a statewide occupational median cannot establish that worker's pay increase.

For aerospace and defense, Michigan's 2026 strategy identifies strategic opportunity across defense, aerospace, maritime and space. It supports testing dual-use supplier pathways; it does not establish open jobs or awarded contracts for pilot employers. [MEDC strategy announcement](https://www.michiganbusiness.org/press-releases/2026/02/defense-and-aerospace-strategic-plan/).

Apply role-specific checks before reserving a training seat:

- **Aviation maintenance:** FAA mechanic certification has a formal school/experience and testing route; experience routes generally require 18 months for one rating or 30 months for both. A short manufacturing bridge is not an A&P qualification. [FAA eligibility](https://www.faa.gov/mechanics/become).
- **Export controls:** do not translate “ITAR” into a blanket citizens-only hiring rule. Lawful permanent residents can be U.S. persons for relevant export-control purposes. Have the employer determine access requirements for the actual work and any authorization needed. [DOJ employment and export-control guidance](https://www.justice.gov/crt/immigrant-and-employee-rights-section-hotline).
- **Classified work:** a worker cannot buy a clearance through a course. The sponsoring organization determines the classified need and initiates processing; offer timing and adjudication remain uncertainties. Provide unclassified alternatives while screening requirements. [DCSA questions](https://www.dcsa.mil/Trust-Decision-Adjudications/FAQS-Trust-Decision-Adjudications/).
- **Cybersecurity and quality:** assess the supplier's contract requirements separately from the employee's qualifications. CMMC is a contract-linked organizational requirement, not a universal personal aerospace credential. Validate the particular solicitation and phase. [DFARS clause prescription](https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/204.7504-solicitation-provision-and-contract-clause.). Treat quality-system and customer approvals similarly; training in traceability does not certify a supplier.
- **Licensed work:** Michigan's electrical route requires registered supervised experience, including 8,000 hours and four years for the standard journeyman route; it cannot be compressed into a short bridge. Nursing has examination/endorsement routes with education and other application requirements. Check professional-engineering scope with LARA rather than assuming every industrial engineering title requires the same license. Individual eligibility remains unverified. [Electrical licensing](https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/bcc/sections/licensing-section/exam-lic/electrical-examination-licensing-registration-application-information), [nursing guide](https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/bpl/health/hp-lic-health-prof/nursing/nursing-licensing-guide-and-faqs), [professional engineers](https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/bpl/occ/prof/engineers).

## 3. Prevent separation before subsidizing reemployment

**Proposal:** use the following order, while allowing urgent external matching in parallel when an employer cannot sustain a job.

| Stage | Intervention | Decision gate and worker protection |
|---|---|---|
| Retain current role | Business recovery support, rescheduling, approved reduced-hours plan | Credible work forecast; disclose reduced earnings; preserve required benefits |
| Upgrade/redeploy internally | Paid skill bridge, supervisor mentoring, trial assignment | Named destination role, paid hours, competency milestones and pay review |
| Voluntary employer transfer | Receiving interviews before separation; direct hire with aligned start date | Worker chooses; written offer specifies pay, hours, benefits and contingencies |
| Supported placement | Rapid Response, benefit navigation, funded training and job search | Benefits application help immediately; no guarantee a course yields employment |

Evaluate reduced hours against the realistic alternative rather than presenting it as full wage replacement. For example, **assume** $1,000 weekly wages, a 30% reduction and a $400 eligible weekly UI benefit amount: $700 wages plus $120 proportional benefit equals $820. The $180 gap remains. Actual eligibility, payments and any supplement's effect must be determined by UIA. The [funding matrix](funding.md) documents the current Work Share rules and an important training eligibility conflict.

Employer-to-employer transitions should be **direct, voluntary hiring** rather than an informal labor-lending arrangement. Use the outgoing employer's release date and receiving employer's signed start date to minimize the interval. Document insurance start dates and arrange permitted support for a gap. A temporary staffing/shared-employer model adds payroll, workers' compensation, supervision and benefit responsibilities and should not be the initial pilot default.

Advance hiring commitments improve a cohort's usefulness only when they identify actual positions, conditions and decision dates. Require employers to interview qualified completers and record results; do not label a conditional interview promise a guaranteed job. Shared cohorts can lower delivery cost, but unrelated employers should independently set compensation and make hiring decisions. Avoid wage-fixing, no-poach commitments and exchanges of competitively sensitive future compensation information. [DOJ/FTC worker guidelines](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-doj-antitrust-guidelines-business-activities-affecting-workers).

**Proposal:** the coordinator initially shares only de-identified skills, preferred work radius, availability and worker-authorized requirements. Share names and contact information only for a worker-approved referral. Keep identity, benefit documentation and employer commercial records in access-controlled case systems, separate from the public analytical evidence store. Employers receive only the records necessary for their hiring decisions. Workers can correct records, decline transfers and pursue outside jobs without penalty. Specify retention limits, access logs, grievance routing and breach response before enrollment.

## 4. Public support and financing

Use existing delivery channels instead of creating a competing grant bureaucracy. The [funding matrix](funding.md) documents Work Share, Going PRO, WIOA, Rapid Response, registered apprenticeships, Reconnect and childcare support. Local Michigan Works! determinations and provider schedules are essential. A program's statutory existence, open intake and remaining local dollars are three different facts.

**Proposal:** assign a fiscal lead to approve a cost-by-cost funding plan before services begin. Record payer, eligibility decision, approval date, service period, cost category, amount, employer contribution and reimbursement evidence. Do not promise grants that require future appropriations or a competitive award. Keep core budget revenue at zero until commitments are signed; public awards reduce the sponsor's financing need, not the economic cost of the intervention.

## 5. Students, graduates and attracting people to Michigan

**Job Corps is now an explicit youth pathway.** The [integrated Job Corps chapter](job-corps.md) covers verified eligibility, three Michigan centers, advertised trades, uncertain seat availability, transition coordination and a separately costed 30-person module. Include young people needing secondary education and sustained support alongside college students/graduates. Keep admission, training and actual employment distinct; a long training route cannot serve as immediate wage continuity for most incumbent workers. The chapter's budget and evaluation amend the pilot below without changing its core 200-worker denominator.

**Finding:** the loaded institutional data cover only four institutions and report awards plus separate earnings/completion cohorts. They do not establish who is available for work, actual placements, or Michigan residence. [Audit and institutional coverage](evidence.md).

**Proposal:** invite the four institutions already represented—Lawrence Technological, Rochester Christian, Oakland and Kettering—along with regional community colleges to a demand-led partnership. No participation is confirmed. For each employer requisition, ask institutions for an opt-in candidate pool and relevant coursework/work samples. Use the CIP–SOC crosswalk to find potentially relevant programs, then validate skills directly. Keep degree levels separate and deduplicate people who hold multiple awards.

Offer students paid internships/co-ops tied to named supervisors, real projects and a documented conversion decision date. Give recent graduates paid entry roles with structured mentoring and a 90/180-day skill and wage review. Registered apprenticeships provide paid work, structured learning and progressive wage increases; participation still requires an employer/sponsor opportunity. [Federal apprenticeship model](https://www.apprenticeship.gov/employers/registered-apprenticeship-program).

The optional 40-student module in [the pilot budget](pilot.md) funds paid work rather than counting university completion totals as candidates. Track application, interview, offer, acceptance, first payroll and six/twelve-month Michigan residence separately. Compare retention with a suitable nonparticipant cohort using consented records; neither national earnings nor employment at a remote Michigan-headquartered firm establishes residence.

**Proposal:** recruit returners and interstate candidates only after local recruitment and job redesign establish a persistent need. Advertise a verified role, pay, location, shift and training commitment; provide an accurate household relocation cost worksheet. Offer spouse/partner referrals and voluntary community introductions. A relocation subsidy would be a proposed employer/local/philanthropic expenditure, not an identified universal state entitlement. New arrivals may not immediately qualify for residency-conditioned education assistance.

Screen housing deposits/rent, transit availability by shift, childcare schedules/costs, disability access, broadband for training and emergency expenses during intake. TriShare's operating model provides evidence that childcare affordability is an established workforce policy concern; this report has not estimated a regional causal effect for these barriers. Prioritize support based on participant-reported need and verified prices. Do not presume relocation is better than a local option or place repayment penalties on workers who leave unsafe or unsuitable employment.

## 6. Implementation and accountability

The [90-day roadmap](pilot.md) assigns case, employer, fiscal and evaluation responsibilities. A case moves from risk validation to informed enrollment, task assessment, a retained-role/receiving-job plan, approved financing, paid training when needed, then payroll verification and follow-up. The case owner remains responsible across organizations. Close an unsuccessful case with its reason and continued-service referral rather than omitting it from outcome statistics.

## 7. Economics and evaluation

The [scenario model](scenario-model.json) and [budget](pilot.md) explicitly separate resource costs, assumed outcomes and additional outcomes versus a counterfactual. They are not estimates fitted to the database. The database currently cannot support a causal unemployment-prevention forecast.

**Proposal:** when capacity is constrained, allocate an enhanced early-intervention offer by lottery among eligible, consented cases while everyone retains normal services. Randomize by employer/cohort if shared training creates spillovers; plan sample size and uncertainty before launch. If randomization is infeasible, use a phased rollout with comparable untreated employers/workers, baseline wages, prior employment, occupation, region and layoff risk, and examine pretrends. Record selection and employer closure risks; do not claim a causal result solely from participant satisfaction or before/after employment.

Predefine six-month sustained employment as employed in at least five of six follow-up months and still employed at month six, with job changes allowed. Publish the precise employment evidence and missing-data rules. Measure employment at twelve months separately. Use payroll/employer confirmation and consented worker reports; UI wage data, if authorized, provide quarterly earnings but not daily unemployment or residence by themselves.

| Measure | Proposed definition and denominator |
|---|---|
| Workers served | Unique consented people receiving a substantive service; distinguish screened/referrals |
| Retained current job | Verified continued employment with original employer; report internal moves separately |
| Sustained employment | Above six-month rule among all enrolled; disclose loss to follow-up |
| Jobs demonstrably saved | Estimated additional retained original-employer jobs versus control, with uncertainty; not all placements |
| Unemployment days avoided | Difference in mean days unemployed between comparison groups over a fixed window; also report all days without paid work |
| Earnings continuity | Actual cumulative wages and separately benefits/support, divided by comparable baseline earnings over the follow-up window |
| Wage improvement | Individual hourly pay and total earnings change; control for hours and report inflation sensitivity |
| Employer retention | Original-employer employment at 6/12 months, among all assigned; distinguish employer participation renewal |
| Michigan retention | Consented residence verification at 6/12 months; employment location is a separate field |
| Training completion | Documented completions / all training starters, with reason for withdrawal |
| Cost per sustained outcome | Cash and economic cost / sustained outcomes; separately divide by estimated additional outcomes |

Report subgroup access and outcomes for incumbent/displaced workers, students, graduates, inbound talent, regions and occupations, with privacy-preserving suppression for small groups. Never add overlapping categories. Establish a lawful data-sharing agreement before requesting administrative records; this report does not authorize access. Publish a quarterly dashboard with denominators, cohort entry dates, missingness and confidence intervals. A failed target should prompt redesign, not relabeling participants as jobs saved.
