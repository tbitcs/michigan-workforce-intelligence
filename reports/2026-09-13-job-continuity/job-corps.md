# Job Corps: a Michigan youth-to-employment pathway

Evidence reviewed September 13, 2026. Job Corps is a candidate component of the broader program, alongside incumbent-worker retention and college-to-career pathways. No center partnership, admission, training seat or receiving job is confirmed.

## Verified role and eligibility

Job Corps serves eligible low-income young people generally ages 16–24; disability-related upper-age exceptions may apply. Admissions determines individual eligibility, including status/background requirements; minors may require guardian participation. Do not infer eligibility from unemployment alone, or treat the upper age limit as universal without checking an applicable exception. [Official eligibility guidance, updated December 2025](https://enrollmenthelp.jobcorps.gov/hc/en-us/articles/38111853723917-Am-I-eligible-for-Job-Corps).

The program describes education and career training at no cost to students, with housing, meals, basic health services, supplies and living allowances. These services can address barriers faced by young people who need more support than a short course. A living allowance is **not an incumbent worker's wage replacement**. Federal delivery costs still exist even when participant tuition is zero. [Job Corps benefits](https://www.jobcorps.gov/benefits).

Current admissions information describes open enrollment and a process usually taking about two months, including admission and campus reviews. That is not a guaranteed start date. Training can span eight months to three years according to the current FAQ, so a new applicant should not be promised a completed qualification or placement during the pilot's first 90 days. [Admissions](https://www.jobcorps.gov/admissions-process), [FAQ](https://www.jobcorps.gov/faqs).

## Operational uncertainty and funding treatment

The Department of Labor announced a contractor-center pause in May 2025, and its FY2026 budget document proposed shutdown funding. Those historical announcements/proposals alone do not establish current center closure. Current admissions pages and an official **May 7, 2026** status list show a different operational context; that dated list marks the three Michigan centers active for arrivals. It does not establish September seat capacity or future appropriations. Recheck admissions and dated status before enrolling anyone. [2025 announcement](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/osec/osec20250529?lang=en), [budget proposal](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/general/budget/2026/CBJ-2026-V1-04.pdf), [dated center status](https://prh.jobcorps.gov/Resources/05-07-2026%20All%20Center%20Status.pdf).

Job Corps is a federally administered participant service, **not a cash grant to a Michigan employer consortium**. Do not book its benefits as pilot revenue or reimburse tuition already provided at no cost. A local workforce partner may assess additional eligible services, but the case plan must identify who pays each distinct cost and secure the relevant approvals. Do not assume WIOA or childcare funds can pay costs already provided by the center.

## Proposed 30-person transition module

Create a separate 30-person annual module, conditional on center and employer agreements. It has two streams: incoming youth needing education/training, and current students or recent completers approaching employment. The latter can support earlier job matching; the former requires longer follow-up. This is a planning capacity target, not a count of available students.

1. A youth navigator screens interest and barriers, obtains informed consent and helps the person reach admissions. The center makes eligibility/admission decisions.
2. The center and worker identify actual training milestones and a realistic completion window. The employer lead obtains suitable Michigan requisitions without promising placement.
3. Before completion, arrange worker-approved interviews, paid work where permitted and a documented apprenticeship/job offer. Apply minor-work, safety, licensing and work-based-learning requirements to the actual role.
4. Coordinate housing after center exit, transport by shift, documents, benefits and the first-paycheck interval. Temporary housing support must not duplicate center-provided housing.
5. Confirm first payroll and follow up at 30/90 days and 6/12 months after job start. Training participation is an intermediate outcome, not a job saved.

**Assumed incremental local budget:** $45,000 for 0.5 loaded FTE navigation, 30 × $1,000 transition supports, $10,000 employer coordination/evaluation, plus 10% contingency: **$93,500**. Federal center operating costs are unknown and excluded; this is not the full social cost of Job Corps. No public funding award is assumed.

Low/base/high planning scenarios assume 10/18/24 of the 30 achieve sustained Michigan employment after transition, compared with 8/10/12 without the enhanced local module. Incremental outcomes are 2/8/12, producing incremental-local-cash costs of $46,750/$11,687.50/$7,791.67 per additional outcome. These are unestimated assumptions; measurement starts from actual job entry and may extend beyond the pilot year. They cannot be compared directly with full Job Corps program cost-effectiveness or the incumbent pilot.

## Program recommendations and accountability

The core 200-worker retention budget remains $1,408,000 cash. Adding the separately funded 40-student module ($293,440) and 30-person Job Corps module ($93,500) yields **$1,794,940 local cash**, plus $480,000 core employer training time: **$2,274,940 in specified resources**, excluding federal Job Corps operating costs and other stated exclusions. Deduplicate anyone eligible for more than one track; do not claim 270 distinct people unless enrollment records establish that fact.

The first 30 days should secure center contacts, current program/arrival confirmation and consented referral terms. By day 60, identify near-completion candidates and employer interviews; by day 90, verify any actual starts and report incoming-trainee progress separately. Youth representation belongs in governance. Participation is voluntary; declining residence or a job cannot justify coercion or an unsupported benefit penalty.

Track screened, referred, admitted, arrived, credentialed, offered, employed and sustained-employed as separate stages. Track Michigan residence directly, not from center attendance. Use a comparison of enhanced transition support versus usual services; do not withhold normal Job Corps services. Center-level national reporting exists, but no Michigan center causal placement or retention estimate was established here. [Official outcome-report inventory](https://www.jobcorps.gov/reports).
