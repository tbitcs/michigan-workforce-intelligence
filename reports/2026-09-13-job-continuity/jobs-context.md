# Jobs and economic outlook: Macomb, Oakland, Wayne and Michigan

This is the **primary evidence report**. Job continuity, Job Corps and talent programs are supplemental proposals, released alongside it. Observation periods differ by source; the build timestamp is not a claim that all data describe today. The generated tables and charts use the audited snapshot, with source identifiers and transformations retained.

## How to interpret the jobs dashboard

LAUS measures residents in the labor force, employment and unemployment. County/state comparisons here use unadjusted series consistently; evaluate the same month a year earlier before attributing a monthly swing to economic deterioration. Michigan CES measures workplace payroll jobs, seasonally adjusted, in thousands. QCEW covers unemployment-insurance-covered payroll jobs and wages; its March employment reference and quarterly wage reference differ. **A net payroll decline is not a count of layoffs**, because hires, quits, closures, expansions and other changes coexist.

Use county rates and labor-force levels together. A lower unemployment rate accompanied by labor-force withdrawal is not necessarily a successful labor market. County residence does not identify workplace county; use commuting data before designing employer transfers. Do not add the three counties to the state total or combine residents and payroll jobs into one employment number. [BLS LAUS](https://www.bls.gov/lau/), [CES](https://www.bls.gov/sae/), [QCEW methods](https://www.bls.gov/cew/).

## Economic conditions that can affect unemployment

| Layer | Official source and useful measure | Use / current limit |
|---|---|---|
| Realized labor market | LAUS, CES, QCEW; state/county series in this release | Observe employment, participation and payroll changes with release lag |
| Gross job flows and turnover | [BLS BED](https://www.bls.gov/bdm/), [state JOLTS](https://www.bls.gov/jlt/), [Census QWI](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/qwi.html) | Distinguish establishment gains/losses from hires/separations; full current county flow history is not yet established |
| Near-term displacement | [DOL UI claims](https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/claims.asp), [Michigan WARN](https://www.michigan.gov/leo/bureaus-agencies/wd/data-public-notices/warn-notices) | Claims/covered notices miss some workers; employer advance signals can arrive earlier |
| Purchasing power | BLS CPI, BEA regional price parities, ACS earnings/rents | CPI changes over time; RPP compares places; household costs require individual inputs |
| Demand and production | [Federal Reserve industrial production/capacity](https://www.federalreserve.gov/datadownload/), [BEA regional GDP/income](https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gdp-county-metro-and-other-areas), [Census business formations](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/) | National/state context; business applications are not jobs and GDP is not household income |
| Finance and household resilience | [Federal Reserve credit/rates](https://www.federalreserve.gov/datadownload/), [New York Fed household debt](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc) | Credit conditions may affect spending/investment; not a causal county layoff score |
| Housing, energy and mobility | [HUD rents](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/fmr.html), [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/opendata/), [ACS](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs), [LODES](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/) | Rent/commute/energy exposure; key/endpoint and source-specific access still required for catalog-only feeds |
| Expectations and market sentiment | [New York Fed SCE](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/sce), [Fed policy/market expectations](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/survey-market-expectations) | National expectations, not Michigan unemployment forecasts; no proprietary market series silently redistributed |

This is a broad monitoring framework, not a claim to model every determinant of unemployment. Track trade policy, interest-sensitive auto demand, technology/product changes, public procurement, demographics, participation barriers and employer order books alongside measured indicators. A credible warning requires corroboration and human review, not a single index threshold.

## Sentiment: expectations versus outcomes

The New York Fed's August 2026 survey, released September 8, reports deteriorating unemployment/job-finding expectations alongside some improvement in respondents' job-loss/quit expectations. One-year inflation expectations were 3.6%. These are national survey perceptions; they cannot establish the probability that a Michigan worker will be laid off. Keep survey field dates and question definitions with any trend. [Original release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260908).

Use this official Reserve source for the current sentiment discussion. University of Michigan consumer sentiment and private confidence/market indexes may be useful **candidate sources**, but their licensing and redistribution terms must be reviewed before automation. An index hosted by FRED does not automatically become public-domain data. No social-media sentiment or synthetic recession probability is presented as observed evidence.

## Interstate purchasing power and talent attraction

The companion charts compare Michigan with California, Texas, North Carolina, Ohio, Indiana, Illinois and Wisconsin. BEA's latest reviewed price-parity release describes 2024 and revises historical estimates; ACS earnings/rent comparisons use 2024 one-year data to match. The historical price series is from one current vintage, not an unreconciled splice of old news releases. [BEA RPP](https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area), [ACS](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs).

The calculation **median earnings / (RPP / 100)** is a purchasing-power screening benchmark. Median earnings include workers with different hours and occupations; it is not a wage offer, household disposable income or causal relocation gain. ACS margins of error are retained in the data; do not overinterpret small differences. RPP itself reflects a regional consumption basket, not a particular family's spending. A lower price level can coexist with lower wages.

For a real candidate, compare written job offers in the same occupation/experience level, actual hours/benefits, taxes, housing tenure, insurance, childcare, transportation and moving expenses. A useful first-pass break-even Michigan offer is **origin offer × Michigan RPP / origin RPP**, using the same year; then replace broad-price assumptions with household quotes. Do not promise savings based solely on the state average. Metropolitan conditions within Michigan differ, and RPP does not provide a household-specific county budget.

## Past, present and future

Historical charts show observed releases and gaps; the latest observation is the latest in the captured source, not automatically the report date. CPI and price parity are different concepts: inflation can accelerate in a place whose price level remains below another state's. County/metro CPI cannot be used to rank cost levels between places.

Published occupation projections in the supplemental report cover 2024–2034 and are not active vacancies or short-term forecasts. Future economic paths remain **scenarios** unless tied to a dated official forecast. Do not extrapolate a smooth trend through recessions, strikes, pandemics or policy breaks. A fixed 11-year automotive cycle is an unverified hypothesis; this release does not assert it as a law or use it to schedule layoffs. Longer automotive production/employment/order histories and backtesting would be needed to evaluate periodicity.

For planning, stress-test lower auto orders, stronger defense/industrial demand, energy-price shocks, tighter credit and higher housing costs. Define assumptions and compare intervention capacity under each; do not label these stress tests predictions. Receiving jobs and employer-reported downsizing dates can inform action more directly than a general cycle story.

## County-specific operational implications

**Macomb:** use actual county employment/payroll trends and local employer confirmations to assess industrial exposure; APEX and Velocity are candidate business-diversification/coordination partners, not guaranteed job creators. **Oakland:** distinguish residents' employment from workplace demand and university awards; use verified receiving roles and commute feasibility. **Wayne:** distinguish Detroit city from Wayne County and the larger metro; validate access by shift and local workforce service jurisdiction. **Michigan:** compare the counties with the statewide trend while maintaining a statewide referral route for occupations and communities outside Southeast Michigan.

The [pilot partner and employer-signal companion](partners-and-signals.md) explains how these observations could support voluntary advance redeployment. A job-saving proposal is not evidence that a job was saved. The release includes both so users can review the analysis before considering an intervention.
