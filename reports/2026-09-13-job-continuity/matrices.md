# Occupation-transition and regional skills-gap matrices

September 13, 2026. These are **pathway proposals**, not verified vacancies or measured worker deficits. Training time and tuition below are **planning assumptions per person**, except the two explicitly sourced provider durations. Exclude wages, living costs and licensing delays from tuition estimates. For occupations not represented above, use the same assessment: actual receiving job → essential tasks → demonstrated worker skills → shortest credible paid bridge → offer and income check. This covers all occupational groups operationally; this report does not claim to enumerate or validate every SOC occupation.

## Wage comparison and regional matrix

**Finding:** O*NET's Michigan wage views identify these as 2025 BLS wage estimates. State annual median machinist pay is $52,360 versus $64,250 for industrial machinery mechanics. The $11,890 difference (about 22.7%) is a cross-occupation benchmark, not a causal return to training. Their respective state 25th–75th ranges are $46,260–62,300 and $57,310–77,310. [Machinists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/51-4041.00?st=MI), [industrial machinery mechanics](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-9041.00?st=MI).

| Wage geography | Machinist annual median | Machinery mechanic annual median |
|---|---:|---:|
| Detroit metro | $59,310 | $71,100 |
| Grand Rapids metro | $50,110 | $62,620 |
| Kalamazoo metro | $49,440 | $66,340 |
| Battle Creek metro | $48,530 | $71,130 |
| Flint metro | $62,850 | $73,900 |

These metro wage geographies do not equal prosperity regions or local workforce service boundaries. Do not sum them or treat them as vacancy counts. Wage dispersion may reflect experience, industry mix, schedules and sampling; an individual offer can differ substantially.

The following **regional skills-gap hypotheses** guide employer discovery. No regional shortage magnitude is established. Named organizations are prospective referral/provider choices, not committed partners; verify county jurisdiction through the [Michigan Works! service finder](https://www.michiganworks.org/michigan-works-network).

| Region / proposed priority | Skills mismatch to validate | Candidate operating/provider route | Availability, commute and missing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Southeast/Detroit: first pilot cluster | Production skills versus controls/maintenance; automotive engineering versus customer-specific defense requirements | Relevant local Michigan Works! agency; Macomb, Oakland and other colleges; supplier employers | Confirm plant/requisition locations, shift travel and qualifications. Do not equate Detroit metro wages with every Southeast county. |
| West/Grand Rapids: first pilot cluster | Operator-to-setup/CNC and equipment maintenance progression | West Michigan workforce partners, GRCC, employers | Obtain seat/tuition quotes and paid release time; metro wages do not establish rural access. |
| Southwest/Kalamazoo–Battle Creek: first pilot cluster | Maintenance, inspection and production planning capabilities | Relevant Michigan Works! agency, community colleges and receiving employers | Wage benchmarks justify testing adjacent moves, not a shortage claim. Separate the two metros and employer catchments. |
| Mid-Michigan/Lansing–Flint: referral expansion | Engineering/technical redeployment and trades progression | Local workforce agencies, community colleges, employers | Require actual job and worker geography; high Flint mechanic benchmark is not proof of many open jobs. |
| Northern Lower Peninsula: access track | Trades, care and business-service requirements; seasonal-to-stable work options | Local workforce agency and college/employer partnerships | Seasonal hours, distance and housing should be measured case by case; no quantified local gaps verified. |
| Upper Peninsula: access track | Maintenance/trades/care skills and employer-specific credentials | Local workforce agency, regional colleges and employers | Sparse commuting options may require distributed learning or voluntary relocation; provider seats and vacancies unverified. |

**Proposal:** prioritize cases by separation urgency, credible receiving demand, income preserved, worker choice and feasibility. Do not rank regions merely by wage level or graduation count. Before expanding beyond the initial clusters, obtain comparable regional projections, dated vacancies, hiring difficulty evidence, worker opt-ins and provider capacity.
